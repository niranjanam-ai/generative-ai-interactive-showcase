Folio II

The Exploration Begins

Diffusion models are generative AI models capable of creating images from textual descriptions. They work by learning how images can be transformed into noise, and then learning to reverse that process — progressively removing noise until a meaningful image emerges.

In effect, the model is taught to find order inside chaos: much as an excavator brushes sediment away from a buried form until a recognisable shape is revealed.

Folio III

The City Beneath the City

A hypothetical archaeological excavation beneath a modern city reveals the remains of a previously unknown ancient civilization. Diffusion‑based image generation is used here as a visualization tool — an immersive conceptual reconstruction of what that civilization might have looked like when it was alive.

Trench 7 stands in for every excavation site where the surface world and the buried world meet: cranes and scaffolding above, brick, pottery and monumental architecture below.

This is a conceptual AI‑generated reconstruction — not an authentic historical photograph, and not archaeological proof.

Folio IV

The Incantation of Creation

The text prompt supplied to the diffusion model

“Create a cinematic, photorealistic first-person archaeological discovery scene titled ‘The City Beneath the City’. Place the viewer inside an active archaeological excavation beneath a modern city, with dusty gloved hands, excavation tools, pottery fragments and an inscribed clay tablet visible in the foreground. Reveal a vast hidden ancient civilization beneath the excavation, with ancient streets, brick structures, monumental architecture, marketplaces, pottery, sculptures, murals and people engaged in everyday activities. Create a visual transition from partially buried archaeological ruins into a reconstructed living civilization. Use dramatic sunlight entering through the excavation opening, floating dust particles, atmospheric depth, realistic archaeological textures, cinematic lighting, photorealism and an immersive sense of discovery. The viewer should feel as though they have personally uncovered a lost civilization hidden beneath the modern world.”

Folio VII

Why Archaeology?

Archaeology often deals with incomplete evidence — fragments of buildings, pottery, inscriptions, tools, and ruins. Generative image models can be explored as visualization tools, turning descriptive information into conceptual reconstructions that help make fragmentary evidence easier to imagine.

AI reconstruction should support visualization and interpretation. It should not be treated as historical evidence.
