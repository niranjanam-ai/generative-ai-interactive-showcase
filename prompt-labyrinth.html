Role Prompting

What is Role Prompting? Assigning a role, persona, or perspective to an LLM so that its response is better aligned with the intended task, audience, and style.

BEFORE

"Explain NLP."

AFTER

"Act as a beginner-friendly NLP teacher. Explain NLP to an MCA student using one simple real-world example."

ROLE
+
CONTEXT
+
TASK

MORE TARGETED RESPONSE

Better relevance to the actual question asked
Consistent style across a conversation
Audience adaptation — expert vs. beginner tone
Task orientation instead of generic output
Useful for teaching, coding, analysis and support
CRITICAL THINKING A role guides behaviour; it does not guarantee expertise or correctness. The model still needs accurate context and verification.

GPT Prompting Tactics

Six practical tactics that shape how clearly an LLM understands what is being asked. Click a card to expand it.

TACTIC 01
Be Clear
Click to expand ↓
WHAT IT MEANSState the task in unambiguous, specific language.
WHY IT HELPSRemoves guesswork so the model doesn't have to infer intent.
EXAMPLE
Weak: "Explain Python."
Better: "Explain Python functions to a beginner in 5 simple points with one example."
TACTIC 02
Give Context
Click to expand ↓
WHAT IT MEANSSupply background — audience, purpose, prior information.
WHY IT HELPSContext narrows the space of plausible answers.
EXAMPLE
Weak: "Summarise this."
Better: "Summarise this report for a non-technical manager in 3 bullet points."
TACTIC 03
Use Delimiters
Click to expand ↓
WHAT IT MEANSMark input sections clearly with quotes, dashes, or tags.
WHY IT HELPSPrevents instructions and content from being confused with each other.
EXAMPLE
Weak: Summarise the following text is late...
Better: Summarise the text between triple quotes: """text"""
TACTIC 04
Specify Output Format
Click to expand ↓
WHAT IT MEANSTell the model exactly how the answer should be structured.
WHY IT HELPSPredictable output is easier to parse and reuse.
EXAMPLE
Weak: "List some fruits."
Better: "List 5 fruits as a numbered list, one word each."
TACTIC 05
Give Examples
Click to expand ↓
WHAT IT MEANSShow sample input/output pairs before the real task.
WHY IT HELPSDemonstrates the expected pattern instead of describing it abstractly.
EXAMPLE
Weak: "Classify sentiment."
Better: "Great! → Positive. Terrible. → Negative. Now classify: 'It was okay.'"
TACTIC 06
Iterate and Refine
Click to expand ↓
WHAT IT MEANSTreat the first response as a draft and refine the prompt.
WHY IT HELPSPrompting is rarely perfect on the first attempt.
EXAMPLE
First: "Write a bio."
Refined: "Rewrite that bio in a more formal tone, under 50 words."

Zero-Shot vs Few-Shot

Two paths through the same classification task. Toggle between them to compare.

NO EXAMPLES PROVIDED
Classify this review as Positive, Negative or Neutral:
"The camera quality is excellent."
OUTPUTPOSITIVE
ZERO-SHOT TRAITS
  • Fast to write and run
  • Simple — no example curation needed
  • No examples required
  • May be ambiguous on edge cases
EXAMPLES PROVIDED BEFORE THE TASK
"The product is excellent." → Positive
"The product stopped working." → Negative
"The product arrived yesterday." → Neutral
"The camera quality is excellent." → ?
FEW-SHOT TRAITS
  • Demonstrates the expected pattern directly
  • Useful for ambiguous or nuanced tasks
  • Uses more tokens / context window
  • Chosen examples influence performance

LLM vs Traditional ML

Two different systems for the same underlying problem: assigning categories to text.

LLM

  • Zero-Shot
  • Few-Shot
  • Semantic Understanding
  • Flexible
  • Less Task-Specific Training

Understands meaning and context without needing a large labelled dataset for every new task.

VS

Traditional ML

  • TF-IDF
  • SVM
  • Naïve Bayes
  • Logistic Regression
  • Fast
  • Low Cost

Built for a fixed task, trained on labelled data, and typically cheaper to run at scale.

"WHO WINS?"
IT DEPENDS.

LLMs may be preferable when

  • Labelled data is limited
  • Semantic understanding matters
  • Tasks change frequently
  • Flexible classification is required

Traditional models may be preferable when

  • The classification task is fixed
  • Large-scale inference is required
  • Latency matters
  • Cost needs to remain low
  • Sufficient labelled training data exists
CRITICAL THINKING LLMs are not automatically better simply because they are larger.

Node 06 — The Decision Chamber

Which Path Should I Choose?

A short decision tree for choosing between an LLM-based approach and a traditional classifier.

QUESTION 01
Do I have many labelled examples?
YES →Consider traditional ML / fine-tuning
NO →Try zero-shot or few-shot LLM prompting
QUESTION 02
Is semantic / contextual understanding important?
YES →An LLM may be useful
NO →A traditional classifier may be sufficient
QUESTION 03
Is the system processing huge amounts of fixed-format text?
YES →Consider a specialised classifier
NO →An LLM may provide useful flexibility
"Choose the simplest approach that performs well for the actual task."
